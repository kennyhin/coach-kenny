Genesis 1 — The Creation of the World

The six days, read on their face and, where the text invites it, as a picture of the heart.

Text says what the verse plainly saysOne reading a way of reading it; weigh it yourselfJust a theory an idea to think about, not a teaching

Scripture the verse being studiedCross-reference another passage that proves the point. Turn to it.

01The First Verse Holds It All

GENESIS 1:1

The opening verse names the basic categories of the physical world — time, motion, energy, space, and matter — each carried by a single word.

02The Heart as the Formless Earth

GENESIS 1:2–3

Before the light, the earth is "without form, and void," dark and deep — and it cannot lift itself out of that darkness. It stays that way until God speaks. Read as the heart, it is the same story: emptiness no human effort can cure, until the Word of God enters and divides the light from the dark.

03Light from God

GENESIS 1:3–5 · JOHN 1 · REVELATION 21–22

Light is spoken into being on day one — three days before the sun. The text calls the light "Day" and the darkness "Night," and the same pairing closes the Bible, where God Himself is the light and there is no night at all.

04Evening, Then Morning

GENESIS 1:5, 1:8

In God's reckoning the day runs "the evening and the morning" — dark first, then light. That order is a pattern for the life of faith: the things of God often begin hard and dark but end in light, while the things of the world begin sweet and bright but end in darkness.

05The Firmament & the Waters

GENESIS 1:6–8 · 1:17 · REVELATION 4:6

God sets a firmament — the Hebrew raqia, a "beaten-out expanse" or vault — in the middle of the waters, dividing the waters above from the waters below. He calls it "Heaven," and the sun, moon and stars are set inside it (v. 17). The expanse is vast and open; its firm part is a thin shell at the very top.

Scripture for this section

Cross-referenceRevelation 4:6 KJV

“And before the throne there was a sea of glass like unto crystal…”

Cross-referenceRevelation 15:2 KJV

“And I saw as it were a sea of glass mingled with fire…”

06Land, Sea & "After His Kind"

GENESIS 1:9–13

On the third day the waters gather into seas, dry land appears, and the earth brings forth grass, herbs and fruit trees — each "after his kind, whose seed is in itself." That short phrase, repeated through the chapter, draws a hard line: life reproduces within its kind. A dog yields dogs, never a cat.

Key points · Genesis 1:9–13

Text says

Ten times: “after his kind”

Count it. In Genesis 1 the phrase “after his kind” (or “after their kind”) appears ten times in five verses: once in verse 11, twice in 12, twice in 21, twice in 24, and three times in 25. God says it again and again so nobody misses it. Life reproduces within its kind. You get many breeds of dog, but a dog never gives birth to a cat. Paul uses the same idea: every seed gets “his own body,” and each kind of flesh stays its own.

ScriptureGenesis 1:25 KJV

“And God made the beast of the earth after his kind, and cattle after their kind, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind: and God saw that it was good.”

Cross-reference1 Corinthians 15:38–39 KJV

“But God giveth it a body as it hath pleased him, and to every seed his own body. All flesh is not the same flesh: but there is one kind of flesh of men, another flesh of beasts, another of fishes, and another of birds.”

One reading

Two meanings of “earth”

In verse 1, “the earth” is the whole world God created. In verse 10, God gives the name “Earth” to the dry land. Scripture uses both senses. Job says God hangs the earth (the world) on nothing. Hannah says the earth (the land) sits on the LORD’s pillars. Once you ask which “earth” a verse means, the two verses don’t contradict each other. Genesis 1 sets up the difference on its first page.

ScriptureGenesis 1:10 KJV

“And God called the dry land Earth; and the gathering together of the waters called he Seas: and God saw that it was good.”

Cross-referenceJob 26:7 KJV

“He stretcheth out the north over the empty place, and hangeth the earth upon nothing.”

Cross-reference1 Samuel 2:8 KJV

“…for the pillars of the earth are the LORD’s, and he hath set the world upon them.”

One reading

The land came up out of the water

God doesn’t say “let there be land.” He says gather the waters “unto one place” and “let the dry land appear.” So the land was already there, under the water. One reading: the earth of verse 1 had been overflowed with water before the six days began, which is why verse 2 finds it dark and covered by “the deep.” Peter speaks of a world that “being overflowed with water, perished,” and Jeremiah uses the exact words “without form, and void.”

ScriptureGenesis 1:9 KJV

“And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so.”

Cross-reference2 Peter 3:5–6 KJV

“For this they willingly are ignorant of, that by the word of God the heavens were of old, and the earth standing out of the water and in the water: Whereby the world that then was, being overflowed with water, perished:”

Cross-referenceJeremiah 4:23 KJV

“I beheld the earth, and, lo, it was without form, and void; and the heavens, and they had no light.”

Text says

God’s word is plain on purpose

“Kind” is everyday language. A child can tell a horse from a cat. God tells the story of creation in words anyone can follow, so keep His own wording. His Word gives understanding “unto the simple,” and Paul warns about “science falsely so called.”

Cross-referencePsalm 119:130 KJV

“The entrance of thy words giveth light; it giveth understanding unto the simple.”

Cross-reference1 Timothy 6:20 KJV

“O Timothy, keep that which is committed to thy trust, avoiding profane and vain babblings, and oppositions of science falsely so called:”

07Lights & the Named Stars

GENESIS 1:14–19 · PSALM 147:4

On the fourth day God sets the sun, moon and stars in the firmament for signs, seasons, days and years — and almost in passing, "he made the stars also." What science counts with computers over lifetimes, God both numbers and names.

Scripture for this section

ScriptureGenesis 1:16 KJV

“And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also.”

Cross-referencePsalm 147:4 KJV

“He telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names.”

Cross-referenceIsaiah 40:26 KJV

“Lift up your eyes on high, and behold who hath created these things, that bringeth out their host by number: he calleth them all by names by the greatness of his might, for that he is strong in power; not one faileth.”

Key points · Genesis 1:14–19

Text says

Plants made one day before the sun

Plants come on day three. The sun comes on day four. Between them is a single “evening and morning.” If each day were a long age, plants would have waited ages for sunlight. As written, they waited one day, and God held them up the whole time. The order isn’t a problem for the One who keeps all things together. Exodus 20:11 repeats it plainly: “in six days.”

ScriptureGenesis 1:13 KJV

“And the evening and the morning were the third day.”

ScriptureGenesis 1:19 KJV

“And the evening and the morning were the fourth day.”

Cross-referenceExodus 20:11 KJV

“For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it.”

Cross-referenceColossians 1:17 KJV

“And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.”

One reading

The lights take over a job God already did

On day one, God himself “divided the light from the darkness” (v.4). On day four the sun, moon and stars are given that same job: “to divide the light from the darkness” (v.18). So the light of day one was a different light from the sun. Scripture ends the same way. In God’s city there is “no night there,” and the opposite of His presence is “outer darkness.” Light is where God is.

ScriptureGenesis 1:4 KJV

“And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness.”

ScriptureGenesis 1:18 KJV

“And to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness: and God saw that it was good.”

Cross-referenceRevelation 21:25 KJV

“And the gates of it shall not be shut at all by day: for there shall be no night there.”

Cross-referenceMatthew 8:12 KJV

“But the children of the kingdom shall be cast out into outer darkness: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.”

Text says

Signs, seasons, days and years

The lights are clocks, but they are also signs. Jesus said people could read the sky for tomorrow’s weather and still miss “the signs of the times,” and He said there will be signs in the sun, moon and stars before His return. Man’s calendars drift and get corrected; God’s timing doesn’t. That is also why Scripture gives the times and seasons to watch, but not a date to set.

ScriptureGenesis 1:14 KJV

“And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:”

Cross-referenceMatthew 16:2–3 KJV

“He answered and said unto them, When it is evening, ye say, It will be fair weather: for the sky is red. And in the morning, It will be foul weather to day: for the sky is red and lowring. O ye hypocrites, ye can discern the face of the sky; but can ye not discern the signs of the times?”

Cross-referenceLuke 21:25 KJV

“And there shall be signs in the sun, and in the moon, and in the stars; and upon the earth distress of nations, with perplexity; the sea and the waves roaring;”

Cross-referenceActs 1:7 KJV

“And he said unto them, It is not for you to know the times or the seasons, which the Father hath put in his own power.”

08Life After Its Kind

GENESIS 1:20–25

Days five and six fill the world: sea creatures and birds, then land animals and creeping things — and over and over the text repeats the same refrain, "after his kind," with the blessing "be fruitful, and multiply." The repetition is the point.

Key points · Genesis 1:20–25

One reading

Born of water, and of the Spirit

On day five the first living creatures come out of the waters. Jesus later tells Nicodemus a man must be “born of water and of the Spirit.” Nicodemus only knew one birth, the physical one, so Jesus explains two: “that which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.” One reading: “born of water” is natural birth (every baby comes out of the water of the womb) and “born of the Spirit” is the new birth. Verse 6 is Jesus’ own explanation of verse 5.

ScriptureGenesis 1:20 KJV

“And God said, Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life, and fowl that may fly above the earth in the open firmament of heaven.”

Cross-referenceJohn 3:3 KJV

“Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.”

Cross-referenceJohn 3:5–6 KJV

“Jesus answered, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God. That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.”

One reading

“Abundantly”: rivers of living water

The waters don’t just trickle out life; they bring it forth “abundantly.” Jesus uses the same picture for the Holy Spirit in a believer: not a drop but “rivers of living water.” John says directly, “this spake he of the Spirit.” When God made the seas full of life, He already had the Spirit-filled life in mind.

Cross-referenceJohn 7:38–39 KJV

“He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water. (But this spake he of the Spirit, which they that believe on him should receive: for the Holy Ghost was not yet given; because that Jesus was not yet glorified.)”

Text says

Great whales, and Jonah

The first sea creatures named are “great whales.” Jonah says God prepared “a great fish,” and Jesus calls it “the whale.” God names His creatures in His own plain words. Jesus trusted Jonah’s story enough to make it the sign of His own death and resurrection.

ScriptureGenesis 1:21 KJV

“And God created great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which the waters brought forth abundantly…”

Cross-referenceJonah 1:17 KJV

“Now the LORD had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.”

Cross-referenceMatthew 12:40 KJV

“For as Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale’s belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth.”

Text says

He spake, and it was done

Life doesn’t drift into being over ages. In Genesis it comes the moment God speaks, all at once and complete.

Cross-referencePsalm 33:9 KJV

“For he spake, and it was done; he commanded, and it stood fast.”

Just a theory

Four lifeless days, then life

Peter says “one day is with the Lord as a thousand years.” Here is a theory to think about, not a teaching. In days one to four there is no living creature. Life arrives on day five, out of the waters. Roughly four thousand years passed from Adam to Christ, and then the Son came “when the fulness of the time was come” to give everlasting life. It’s a picture to think about. It doesn’t make the days of Genesis 1 long ages, since they are still marked “evening and morning.”

Cross-reference2 Peter 3:8 KJV

“But, beloved, be not ignorant of this one thing, that one day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day.”

Cross-referenceGalatians 4:4 KJV

“But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law,”

Cross-referenceJohn 3:16 KJV

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

09God's Image & the Trinity

GENESIS 1:26–27

At the summit of creation God says, "Let us make man in our image" — a plural — yet verse 27 says "God created man in his own image" — a singular. One God speaking as more than one person: a first glimpse of the triune God, on the very first page.

Scripture for this section

ScriptureGenesis 1:26 KJV

“And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness…”

ScriptureGenesis 1:27 KJV

“So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.”

ScriptureGenesis 3:22 KJV

“And the LORD God said, Behold, the man is become as one of us…”

Cross-referenceIsaiah 6:8 KJV

“Also I heard the voice of the Lord, saying, Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? Then said I, Here am I; send me.”

Cross-reference1 John 5:7 KJV

“For there are three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one.”

Key points · Genesis 1:26–27

Text says

“Him,” then “them”: man means all mankind

Verse 26 says “let us make man” and then “let them have dominion.” Verse 27 says God created “him,” and then “male and female created he them.” In the King James English, “man” often means the whole human race. Genesis 5 says it outright: God made them male and female and “called their name Adam.”

ScriptureGenesis 5:1–2 KJV

“This is the book of the generations of Adam. In the day that God created man, in the likeness of God made he him; Male and female created he them; and blessed them, and called their name Adam, in the day when they were created.”

One reading

Male and female: made to fit

Why does so much of life come in two kinds that fit together? Reproduction is needed for survival from day one. If the two halves had to develop slowly and separately, neither could survive while waiting for the other. A design that has to work all at once points to a mind behind it. Jesus quotes this exact verse: “from the beginning of the creation.”

Cross-referenceMark 10:6 KJV

“But from the beginning of the creation God made them male and female.”

10Dominion & the First Diet

GENESIS 1:28–31 · ISAIAH 11

Mankind is blessed and given dominion — but notice its reach: "over the fish of the sea… the fowl of the air… and over all the earth." Not over the sun, moon and stars. And the first diet, for people and animals alike, is plant-based — a peace the prophets say will return.

Scripture for this section

ScriptureGenesis 1:28 KJV

“And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.”

ScriptureGenesis 1:29 KJV

“And God said, Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree, in the which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat.”

ScriptureGenesis 1:30 KJV

“And to every beast of the earth, and to every fowl of the air, and to every thing that creepeth upon the earth, wherein there is life, I have given every green herb for meat: and it was so.”

Cross-referenceIsaiah 11:6–7 KJV

“The wolf also shall dwell with the lamb, and the leopard shall lie down with the kid; and the calf and the young lion and the fatling together; and a little child shall lead them. And the cow and the bear shall feed; their young ones shall lie down together: and the lion shall eat straw like the ox.”

Key points · Genesis 1:28–31

One reading

Reaching for heaven is an old ambition

Man’s rule stops at the earth. “The heaven, even the heavens, are the LORD’s.” Notice what happens when creatures reach past that line. Babel wanted a tower “whose top may reach unto heaven.” Lucifer said, “I will ascend into heaven” (see the next card). In Revelation the dragon and his angels make war in heaven and are thrown down. Grabbing for heaven has always been the enemy’s ambition. Man’s assignment is the earth.

Cross-referencePsalm 115:16 KJV

“The heaven, even the heavens, are the LORD’s: but the earth hath he given to the children of men.”

ScriptureGenesis 11:4 KJV

“And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.”

Cross-referenceRevelation 12:7–9 KJV

“And there was war in heaven: Michael and his angels fought against the dragon; and the dragon fought and his angels, And prevailed not; neither was their place found any more in heaven. And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world: he was cast out into the earth, and his angels were cast out with him.”

One reading

Lucifer ruled the earth before man

Look at who Lucifer was. His throne was below the stars and clouds, since he wanted to “ascend” above them. He had been “in Eden the garden of God.” Now God gives a new creature dominion “over every living thing that moveth upon the earth” (v.28). One reading: his fall can’t fit inside the six days, because everything there is “very good.” It can’t come after them either, because the serpent is already in the garden in Genesis 3. That puts his fall before the six days, between verses 1 and 2.

Cross-referenceIsaiah 14:12–14 KJV

“How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! how art thou cut down to the ground, which didst weaken the nations! For thou hast said in thine heart, I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God: I will sit also upon the mount of the congregation, in the sides of the north: I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will be like the most High.”

Cross-referenceEzekiel 28:13 KJV

“Thou hast been in Eden the garden of God…”

Cross-referenceEzekiel 28:14 KJV

“Thou art the anointed cherub that covereth…”

Cross-referenceEzekiel 28:15 KJV

“Thou wast perfect in thy ways from the day that thou wast created, till iniquity was found in thee.”

ScriptureGenesis 3:1 KJV

“Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made.”

One reading

Fill, then replenish

The fish and birds are told to “fill the waters” (v.22). Adam is told to “replenish the earth” (v.28). Noah, after the flood wiped out the world’s people, hears the same word, “replenish.” And when Israel filled Egypt for the first time, Exodus says “filled.” One reading: God picks His words carefully. “Fill” is for the first time and “replenish” is for filling again, which fits an earlier world before Adam.

ScriptureGenesis 1:22 KJV

“And God blessed them, saying, Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let fowl multiply in the earth.”

ScriptureGenesis 1:28 KJV

“…Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it…”

ScriptureGenesis 9:1 KJV

“And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth.”

Cross-referenceExodus 1:7 KJV

“And the children of Israel were fruitful, and increased abundantly, and multiplied, and waxed exceeding mighty; and the land was filled with them.”

Text says

“Meat” here means food, and it was all plants

In the King James Bible, “meat” means food, and verse 29 defines it as herbs and fruit with seed. Animals get “every green herb.” People and animals both start out eating plants. Eating animals is only given after the flood, in Genesis 9:3. Isaiah looks ahead to a day when the lion eats straw again, so the end of the story brings back its peaceful start.

ScriptureGenesis 9:3 KJV

“Every moving thing that liveth shall be meat for you; even as the green herb have I given you all things.”

Text says

“Very good,” and still beautiful

Each day was “good.” When God looked at everything together, it was “very good.” Even now, after the fall and after Noah’s flood, the world is full of beautiful places. That is His handiwork still showing, and Paul says creation leaves everyone “without excuse.”

ScriptureGenesis 1:31 KJV

“And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.”

Cross-referencePsalm 104:24 KJV

“O LORD, how manifold are thy works! in wisdom hast thou made them all: the earth is full of thy riches.”

Cross-referenceRomans 1:20 KJV

“For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse:”

Text says

Who was Cain afraid of?

Verse 28 gives man dominion over “every living thing that moveth upon the earth.” Later Cain fears that “every one that findeth me shall slay me.” Who was out there? The plain answer is in Genesis 5:4: Adam lived for centuries and “begat sons and daughters,” so the family had grown by then.

ScriptureGenesis 4:14 KJV

“Behold, thou hast driven me out this day from the face of the earth; and from thy face shall I be hid; and I shall be a fugitive and a vagabond in the earth; and it shall come to pass, that every one that findeth me shall slay me.”

ScriptureGenesis 5:4 KJV

“And the days of Adam after he had begotten Seth were eight hundred years: and he begat sons and daughters:”

11The Six Days at a Glance

GENESIS 1:1–2:3

Seen whole, the six days fall into two matching halves. In days one to three God forms the empty, unshaped places; in days four to six He fills them. Day four fills day one, day five fills day two, day six fills day three — and the seventh crowns it all.
