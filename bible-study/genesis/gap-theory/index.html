Genesis 1:1–2 — A Former World, Then the Six Days

One reading of the opening verses: a created heaven and earth, a judged earth under water, and six days that begin when God says, “Let there be light.”

Text says what the verse plainly saysOne reading a way of reading it; weigh it yourselfJust a theory an idea to think about, not a teaching

Scripture the verse being studiedCross-reference another passage that proves the point. Turn to it.

Reading guide

  1. 01Verse 1 is the original creation. Verse 2 is an earth without form, void, and under water. The six days begin at verse 3. This reading still holds the earth under 10,000 years.
  2. 02“And” marks the next event. Verse 2 comes after verse 1. Some read verses 1–3 as one continuous event.
  3. 03“Without form, and void” is judgment language in Jeremiah 4:23–28, not a note that the earth was being prepared.
  4. 04Reading verse 2 as a watery creation process leaves room for evolutionary readings. Reading it as destruction does not.
  5. 05The morning stars and the sons of God were present when the foundations of the earth were laid.
  6. 06God formed the earth to be inhabited, and He created it not in vain.
  7. 07The anointed cherub was in Eden, perfect until iniquity, and his throne was below the stars and the clouds.
  8. 08Sin entered by one man. The cherub had sanctuaries and a kingdom. Adam is given dominion over all the earth.
  9. 09A flood “from the beginning of the creation” overflows “the world that then was,” and the heavens are in the sentence. Noah and the ark are not named.
  10. 10“Fill” and “replenish” are not the same English word. Noah replenishes. Israel filled Egypt the first time.
  11. 11Two objections: “the first earth,” and “death passed upon all men.”
  12. 12Eight reasons, side by side. The last is a connection: hostility, because Adam received a dominion that had been lost.

01What the View Is

GENESIS 1:1–3

This reading does not treat Genesis 1:1–3 as one moment. Verse 1 is the creation of the heaven and the earth. Verse 2 finds that earth without form, void, dark, and covered with waters. The six days begin when God speaks in verse 3. The world before Adam is a separate world from the six days. The days themselves stay evenings and mornings. This reading does not stretch them into millions of years, and it does not fill the space between verse 1 and verse 2 with millions of years either. Counted with the generations that follow Adam, the earth is still held to be under 10,000 years old.

Key points · Genesis 1:1–3

One reading

Three steps, and a world before Adam

What this shows: Verse 1 creates the heaven and the earth. Verse 2 finds the earth without form, void, and under waters. Verse 3 begins the six days (Genesis 1:1–3).

Why it matters: The world before Adam is not the six-day world. Some read verses 1–3 as one continuous event, the creating, the unformed deep, and the first word of light all belonging to the same beginning.

Read the three verses in order and let each line do its own work. Genesis 1:1 is a complete sentence. God created the heaven and the earth. Genesis 1:2 does not repeat that act. It says what the earth was: without form, and void, darkness on the face of the deep, waters under the Spirit of God. Genesis 1:3 is the first command, and the six days are counted from there. One reading: something stands between the created world of verse 1 and the dark waters of verse 2. That former world is not Adam’s world. Some read verses 1–3 as one continuous event: verse 1 titles the work, verse 2 gives the first condition, and verse 3 starts shaping it. That reading is real, and it should be weighed against the lines that follow.

ScriptureGenesis 1:2 KJV

One reading

Under 10,000 years, not millions

What this shows: The six days are still evenings and mornings. “In six days the LORD made heaven and earth” (Exodus 20:11; Genesis 1:5).

Why it matters: This reading does not use a gap to make room for millions of years. With the generations from Adam, the earth is still held to be under 10,000 years old (Genesis 5; Genesis 11).

A gap is not a license to turn the days into ages. Genesis 1:5 closes day one the same way the chapter closes every day: “the evening and the morning.” Exodus 20:11 says the LORD made heaven and earth in six days. Genesis 5 and Genesis 11 count the years of men after Adam. One reading: put the six days and those generations together, and the earth is still under 10,000 years old. The space between verse 1 and verse 2 is not a hidden million-year age.

Text says

What this shows: All things were made by the Word, and by Him all things consist, visible and invisible, thrones and dominions included (John 1:3; Colossians 1:16–17).

Why it matters: The heaven and the earth of Genesis 1:1, and any rule in them, still come from Christ. A former world would not be a world He did not make.

Cross-check: the opening act of Genesis 1:1 has a name in the New Testament. John 1:3 says all things were made by Him, and without Him was not any thing made that was made. Colossians 1:16–17 adds the unseen order: thrones, dominions, principalities, and powers were created by Him and for Him, and by Him all things consist. If this reading is right about a world before Adam, that world was still His.

02“And” Marks Sequence

GENESIS 1:1–5

Verse 2 does not begin with “when,” or with a restatement of verse 1. It begins with “And.” The same word opens the acts that follow, one after another, through the chapter. One reading: “And” marks the next event, so verse 2 happens after verse 1, not at the same time. Some read verses 1–3 as one continuous event.

Key points · Genesis 1:1–5

One reading

Verse 2 is the next event, not the same moment

What this shows: Genesis 1:2 opens with “And,” and so does each act after it (Genesis 1:3–5).

Why it matters: A chain of “And … and …” is how the chapter orders events. Some read verses 1–3 as one continuous event instead.

Put verse 1 next to verse 2 and watch the first word. Verse 1 has no “And.” It states the creation. Verse 2 joins a further line to it: “And the earth was…” Verse 3 joins another: “And God said…” Verse 4 joins another: “And God saw the light, that it was good.” Verse 5 joins the naming, and then the day: “And the evening and the morning were the first day.” One reading: the chapter is not stacking synonyms for a single instant. It is walking forward. Verse 2 happens after verse 1. Some read verses 1–3 as one continuous event, the creation told and then described. Weigh the “And” on its own, in the order the verses stand.

ScriptureGenesis 1:4 KJV

“And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness.”

ScriptureGenesis 1:5 KJV

03“Without Form, and Void” Is Judgment

GENESIS 1:2 · JEREMIAH 4:23–28

The phrase “without form, and void” is not a recipe for getting a world ready. The only other place the King James uses that full phrase is a scene of judgment. Jeremiah sees the earth without form and void, the heavens with no light, no man, the birds fled, a fruitful place turned to wilderness, and cities broken down at the presence of the LORD and by His fierce anger.

Key points · Jeremiah 4:23–28

Text says

The same phrase is a scene of wrath

What this shows: Jeremiah beholds the earth “without form, and void,” the heavens with no light, and the land desolate by the LORD’s fierce anger (Jeremiah 4:23–28).

Why it matters: Set beside Genesis 1:2, the phrase reads as judgment. Some read it as the earth simply not finished yet.

Jeremiah’s picture has a before and an after. There had been a fruitful place. There had been cities. He beholds them broken down. The heavens have no light. There is no man. The birds are fled. The LORD says the whole land shall be desolate, yet He will not make a full end, and the earth mourns because He has spoken. One reading: Genesis 1:2 uses the phrase for an earth already brought to that condition, not for raw material waiting its first shape. Some read “without form, and void” as preparation: the earth not yet ordered, and the six days about to order it. The Jeremiah passage shows what the phrase looks like when the Bible uses it again.

Cross-referenceJeremiah 4:23 KJV

“I beheld the earth, and, lo, it was without form, and void; and the heavens, and they had no light.”

04A Watery Process, or a Ruin

GENESIS 1:2–3

How verse 2 is read decides what kind of story the six days are. Some read the dark deep, the moving Spirit, and the waters as the first stage of a creation process, the earth taking shape out of the water. That reading fits evolutionary accounts of a world slowly forming. Reading verse 2 as destruction does not. A judged earth is not a nursery for deep time, and this reading still keeps the whole earth under 10,000 years.

Key points · Genesis 1:2–3

One reading

Destruction does not feed a long creation story

What this shows: Some read Genesis 1:2 as a watery creation process. This reading takes the same verse as an earth already without form and void.

Why it matters: The second reading does not leave the six days, or the space before them, open to millions of years (Genesis 1:3; Exodus 20:11).

If verse 2 is the earth in the middle of being made — water, darkness, and a Spirit moving while the world slowly comes up — then a long process can be laid over the verse. Evolutionary readings use that kind of watery beginning. If verse 2 is the earth after judgment, the way Jeremiah 4:23 uses the phrase, the verse is an end, not a start. The new start is verse 3: “Let there be light.” One reading: that is why this view answers evolutionary readings without borrowing their ages. The earth in this reading is still under 10,000 years old.

ScriptureGenesis 1:2–3 KJV

“And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters. And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.”

05Angels at the Foundation

JOB 38:4–7

When God lays the foundations of the earth, Job is not there. Someone is. The morning stars sing together, and all the sons of God shout for joy. One reading: the sons of God are the angels, already present at the foundation, which is the creation in Genesis 1:1, before the six days that begin at verse 3.

Key points · Job 38:4–7

One reading

The sons of God were already present

What this shows: At the laying of the earth’s foundations, the morning stars sang and all the sons of God shouted (Job 38:4–7).

Why it matters: Elsewhere “the sons of God” come before the LORD, and Satan comes among them (Job 1:6). One reading: angels witnessed Genesis 1:1, before Adam and before the six days.

The questions in Job 38:4–6 are about measures, a line, foundations, and a corner stone. Verse 7 dates the scene: it was “when” the morning stars sang and all the sons of God shouted. Mankind is not in that choir. Adam is not made until day six. Job 1:6 uses “the sons of God” for those who present themselves before the LORD, with Satan among them. One reading: the angels were present when the foundations were laid. That places them at the creation of verse 1, with a history already underway before Genesis 1:3.

Cross-referenceJob 38:4–7 KJV

“Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? declare, if thou hast understanding. Who hath laid the measures thereof, if thou knowest? or who hath stretched the line upon it? Whereupon are the foundations thereof fastened? or who laid the corner stone thereof; When the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy?”

Cross-referenceJob 1:6 KJV

“Now there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan came also among them.”

06Made to Be Inhabited

ISAIAH 45:18 · GENESIS 1:2

Isaiah 45:18 says God formed the earth and made it, established it, created it not in vain, and formed it to be inhabited. Genesis 1:2 says the earth was without form, and void. The Hebrew word behind “without form” in that verse, and behind “in vain” in Isaiah, is the same word, tohu. One reading: God did not create the earth in the condition verse 2 describes. He formed it to be inhabited. Verse 2 is a later condition.

Key points · Isaiah 45:18

One reading

“Not in vain” against “without form, and void”

What this shows: God created the earth not in vain, and formed it to be inhabited (Isaiah 45:18).

Why it matters: Genesis 1:2 is an earth without form and void. One reading: that cannot be the condition in which He created it.

Isaiah piles up the verbs: created the heavens, formed the earth, made it, established it, created it not in vain, formed it to be inhabited. The earth of Genesis 1:2 is not inhabited. It is without form, and void, with darkness on the deep. Jeremiah uses that same English phrase for judgment, and the Hebrew tohu stands behind “without form” there and behind “in vain” in Isaiah. One reading: verse 1 is the creating. Verse 2 is the earth no longer in the condition God says He gave it. He formed it to be inhabited. Something left it empty.

Cross-referenceIsaiah 45:18 KJV

“For thus saith the LORD that created the heavens; God himself that formed the earth and made it; he hath established it, he created it not in vain, he formed it to be inhabited: I am the LORD; and there is none else.”

ScriptureGenesis 1:2 KJV

“And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.”

07Lucifer on the Earth Before His Fall

EZEKIEL 28:12–15 · ISAIAH 14:12–14

The lament over the king of Tyrus does not stay inside a human city. The one addressed was in Eden the garden of God, the anointed cherub that covereth, perfect in his ways from the day he was created, till iniquity was found in him. Isaiah’s Lucifer says he will ascend into heaven, exalt his throne above the stars of God, and ascend above the heights of the clouds. Ascending means his place was below those things.

Key points · Ezekiel 28:12–15

One reading

In Eden, perfect, until iniquity was found

What this shows: The lament calls him king, full of wisdom, perfect in beauty, in Eden the garden of God, the anointed cherub, perfect until iniquity was found (Ezekiel 28:12–15).

Why it matters: One reading: this is not only the human king of Tyre. A created cherub in Eden, perfect and then fallen, belongs to a world before Adam’s fall. Some read the whole chapter as the king of Tyre only.

The passage opens as a lament upon the king of Tyrus. Then the lines pass what a city-king can carry. He was in Eden the garden of God. Every precious stone was his covering. He was the anointed cherub that covereth, set so by God, upon the holy mountain of God. He was perfect from the day he was created, till iniquity was found in him. One reading: the king of Tyre is the near address, and the anointed cherub is the further one. That cherub was on the earth, in Eden, before iniquity. Some keep every line on the human king. The words “created,” “cherub,” and “Eden the garden of God” are what the other reading refuses to flatten.

Cross-referenceEzekiel 28:12–15 KJV

“Son of man, take up a lamentation upon the king of Tyrus, and say unto him, Thus saith the Lord GOD; Thou sealest up the sum, full of wisdom, and perfect in beauty. Thou hast been in Eden the garden of God; every precious stone was thy covering, the sardius, topaz, and the diamond, the beryl, the onyx, and the jasper, the sapphire, the emerald, and the carbuncle, and gold: the workmanship of thy tabrets and of thy pipes was prepared in thee in the day that thou wast created. Thou art the anointed cherub that covereth; and I have set thee so: thou wast upon the holy mountain of God; thou hast walked up and down in the midst of the stones of fire. Thou wast perfect in thy ways from the day that thou wast created, till iniquity was found in thee.”

One reading

He had to ascend, so he was below

What this shows: Lucifer says he will ascend into heaven, above the stars of God, and above the heights of the clouds (Isaiah 14:12–14).

Why it matters: A throne that must go up to reach the stars and the clouds is a throne under them, on the earth, before the fall that cuts him down to the ground.

Count the direction of the five “I will” lines. Ascend into heaven. Exalt my throne above the stars. Sit upon the mount of the congregation. Ascend above the clouds. Be like the most High. You do not ascend into a place you already occupy, and you do not exalt a throne above the stars if the throne is already above them. One reading: Lucifer’s domain was below heaven, below the stars, and below the clouds. He is then “cut down to the ground.” That fits a ruler of the earth in the world before Genesis 1:2, not a being who started in Adam’s garden with no realm of his own.

Cross-referenceIsaiah 14:12–14 KJV

“How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! how art thou cut down to the ground, which didst weaken the nations! For thou hast said in thine heart, I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God: I will sit also upon the mount of the congregation, in the sides of the north: I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will be like the most High.”

08Why He Cannot Share Adam’s Timeline

ROMANS 5:12 · EZEKIEL 28:12, 28:14, 28:18 · GENESIS 1:26, 1:28

Four lines will not sit inside Adam’s week at the same time. Sin enters the world by one man. The cherub defiles his sanctuaries, so he had sanctuaries. He is called king and the anointed cherub, so he held rule. Adam is then given dominion over all the earth. One reading: the cherub’s rule and fall belong to the world before verse 2. They cannot be chapters of Adam’s timeline.

Key points · Romans 5:12

Text says

By one man sin entered

What this shows: Sin entered into the world by one man, and death by sin, and death passed upon all men (Romans 5:12).

Why it matters: One reading: the cherub’s iniquity is not the entrance of sin into Adam’s world. That entrance is by one man. The cherub’s fall sits earlier.

The verse names the gate: “by one man.” It does not say sin entered by an angel, and it does not say death passed upon angels. The men in view are those who sinned in Adam’s line, which the next section takes up as an objection. One reading uses the gate itself. If sin enters Adam’s world by one man, then the iniquity already found in the cherub (Ezekiel 28:15) is not an event inside that world. It happened before the six days, in the world of verse 1.

Cross-referenceRomans 5:12 KJV

“Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:”

Cross-referenceEzekiel 28:15 KJV

“Thou wast perfect in thy ways from the day that thou wast created, till iniquity was found in thee.”

One reading

He had sanctuaries, and he was king

What this shows: He defiled his sanctuaries, and the lament calls him king and the anointed cherub (Ezekiel 28:18; Ezekiel 28:12; 28:14).

Why it matters: Sanctuaries and a kingdom are a domain. Adam is afterward given dominion over all the earth (Genesis 1:26; 1:28).

“Thou hast defiled thy sanctuaries” assumes sanctuaries that were his. A king holds rule. The anointed cherub was set so by God. Then Genesis 1:26 and 1:28 give man dominion over the fish, the fowl, the cattle, every creeping thing, and all the earth. One reading: God does not give “all the earth” to Adam while a fallen cherub still holds it. The cherub’s kingdom was the former world. By the time of the six days, that rule is already lost, and the earth of verse 2 is empty of it.

Cross-referenceEzekiel 28:18 KJV

“Thou hast defiled thy sanctuaries by the multitude of thine iniquities, by the iniquity of thy traffick; therefore will I bring forth a fire from the midst of thee, it shall devour thee, and I will bring thee to ashes upon the earth in the sight of all them that behold thee.”

09A Flood That Touched the Heavens

2 PETER 3:4–7 · 3:10 · 3:13 · REVELATION 21:1

Scoffers say that all things continue as they were from the beginning of the creation. Peter says they are ignorant of this: by the word of God the heavens were of old, and the earth was standing out of the water and in the water, whereby the world that then was, being overflowed with water, perished. The heavens and the earth which are now are kept for fire. Noah is not named. The ark is not named. The heavens, plural, are in the sentence, both in the past and in the future passing away. One reading: this overflow is the water of Genesis 1:2, from the beginning of the creation, not Noah’s flood. Some read 2 Peter 3:6 as Noah’s flood.

Key points · 2 Peter 3:4–13

One reading

“The world that then was” perished by water

What this shows: From the beginning of the creation, the heavens were of old, the earth stood out of the water and in the water, and the world that then was perished, overflowed with water (2 Peter 3:4–6).

Why it matters: Noah and the ark are not mentioned. The heavens are. Some read this flood as Noah’s. This reading ties the overflow to Genesis 1:2, at the beginning.

Notice what the scoffers deny, and what Peter says they ignore. They speak of “the beginning of the creation,” as if nothing has ever broken the continuity. Peter answers with the heavens of old and an earth standing out of the water and in the water. “Whereby” points back to that water: the world that then was perished. Verse 7 then names a different pair, “the heavens and the earth, which are now.” One reading: “which are now” only makes sense if a world before this one has already perished. Noah’s flood is never named here, and Noah’s flood is not said to perish the heavens. Some read verse 6 as Noah’s flood anyway, because it is the flood readers know. Weigh the words that are present: beginning of the creation, heavens of old, the world that then was, and the heavens and the earth which are now.

Cross-reference2 Peter 3:4–7 KJV

“And saying, Where is the promise of his coming? for since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue as they were from the beginning of the creation. For this they willingly are ignorant of, that by the word of God the heavens were of old, and the earth standing out of the water and in the water: Whereby the world that then was, being overflowed with water, perished: But the heavens and the earth, which are now, by the same word are kept in store, reserved unto fire against the day of judgment and perdition of ungodly men.”

Text says

The heavens pass, and new ones are promised

What this shows: The heavens shall pass away, and Peter looks for new heavens and a new earth (2 Peter 3:10; 2 Peter 3:13; Revelation 21:1).

Why it matters: A flood that sits beside “the heavens were of old” is being compared with a future in which the heavens themselves pass. Cross-check: the same Christ who made all things (John 1:3; Colossians 1:16–17) is the one by whom the promise of new heavens stands.

2 Peter 3:10 does not say the earth alone is judged. “The heavens shall pass away with a great noise.” Verse 13 looks past that dissolution: “new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness.” Revelation 21:1 sees the first heaven and the first earth passed away. Cross-check: the pattern is creation, a world that perishes, a world that is now, and a new heaven and earth still promised. Colossians 1:17 says He is before all things, and by Him all things consist. The worlds in Peter’s sequence are His work, including the new one.

Cross-reference2 Peter 3:10 KJV

“But the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night; in the which the heavens shall pass away with a great noise, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat, the earth also and the works that are therein shall be burned up.”

Cross-reference2 Peter 3:13 KJV

“Nevertheless we, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness.”

Cross-referenceRevelation 21:1 KJV

“And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea.”

10Replenish, Set Against Fill

GENESIS 1:22 · 1:28 · GENESIS 9:1 · EXODUS 1:7

The fish and the fowl are told to fill the waters. Adam is told to replenish the earth. After the flood, Noah is told to replenish the earth. When Israel multiplies in Egypt for the first time, the land was filled with them. The King James does not use one English word for all four. Noah Webster’s 1828 dictionary gives replenish two senses: as a transitive verb, “To fill; to stock with numbers or abundance,” illustrated from Genesis 1; as an intransitive verb, “To recover former fullness.”

Key points · Genesis 1:28

One reading

Fill the first time, replenish when filling again

What this shows: Fish and fowl “fill.” Adam and Noah “replenish.” Israel “filled” the land of Egypt (Genesis 1:22; 1:28; Genesis 9:1; Exodus 1:7).

Why it matters: Noah replenishes because a world of people had perished. One reading hears the same word to Adam as a recovering of former fullness, which is the intransitive sense in Webster’s 1828 dictionary. Webster’s transitive sense is simply “to fill.”

Lay the four sentences in a row. The fish have no earlier schools of fish in the text, and the word is “fill.” Israel’s growth in Egypt is a first filling, and the word is “filled.” Noah stands on the far side of a flood that swept the people away, and the word is “replenish.” Adam, in the same chapter as “fill,” is told to “replenish.” One reading: the change of word is deliberate. Webster in 1828 records “to recover former fullness” for the intransitive verb, and “to fill; to stock with numbers or abundance” for the transitive verb, the sense Webster illustrates from Genesis 1. Weigh both. The reading on this page takes Adam’s “replenish” with Noah’s, as a filling again, and takes the fish’s “fill” as a first filling.

ScriptureGenesis 1:22 KJV

“And God blessed them, saying, Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let fowl multiply in the earth.”

ScriptureGenesis 1:28 KJV

“And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.”

ScriptureGenesis 9:1 KJV

“And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth.”

Cross-referenceExodus 1:7 KJV

“And the children of Israel were fruitful, and increased abundantly, and multiplied, and waxed exceeding mighty; and the land was filled with them.”

Text says

Joseph filled the sacks, then filled them again

What this shows: Joseph commands the sacks filled with corn, and later commands them filled again with food (Genesis 42:25; Genesis 44:1).

Why it matters: Both verses say “fill.” The second is still a second time. One reading sets that picture beside “replenish”: a fullness restored, not a first filling only.

The brothers’ sacks are filled in Genesis 42:25. They go home. They come back. Genesis 44:1 commands the steward to fill the men’s sacks with food again, as much as they can carry. The King James uses “fill” both times. What the story shows is the second time. One reading: that is the shape of replenish, a former fullness put back, and it is the shape of the word given to Adam and to Noah rather than the word given to the fish.

ScriptureGenesis 42:25 KJV

“Then Joseph commanded to fill their sacks with corn, and to restore every man’s money into his sack, and to give them provision for the way: and thus did he unto them.”

ScriptureGenesis 44:1 KJV

“And he commanded the steward of his house, saying, Fill the men’s sacks with food, as much as they can carry, and put every man’s money in his sack’s mouth.”

11Two Common Objections

REVELATION 21:1 · ROMANS 5:12

Two verses are often set against this reading. Revelation 21:1 says the first heaven and the first earth pass away. Romans 5:12 says death passed upon all men. Both deserve to be read in the sentences around them.

Key points · Revelation 21:1

One reading

“The first earth” is this earth, set against the new one

What this shows: John sees a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away (Revelation 21:1).

Why it matters: Some read “the first earth” as proof there was never an earlier world. One reading sets “first” against “new,” the earth which is now (2 Peter 3:7) against the one still promised (2 Peter 3:13).

Revelation 21:1 is a contrast inside one vision: new, because first has passed. 2 Peter 3 uses the same contrast: a promise of new heavens and a new earth, after “the world that then was” has already been set apart from “the heavens and the earth, which are now.” One reading: “the first earth” is this present earth, first in the pair that gives way to the new one. It is not a claim that no world perished before the world which is now. Some read the phrase as closing that door. The same chapter of Peter that speaks of the earth which is now also speaks of the world that then was.

Cross-referenceRevelation 21:1 KJV

“And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea.”

Cross-reference2 Peter 3:6–7 KJV

“Whereby the world that then was, being overflowed with water, perished: But the heavens and the earth, which are now, by the same word are kept in store, reserved unto fire against the day of judgment and perdition of ungodly men.”

Cross-reference2 Peter 3:13 KJV

“Nevertheless we, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness.”

One reading

Death passed upon all men, and the verse says “men”

What this shows: Death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned (Romans 5:12).

Why it matters: Some read the verse as ruling out any death, of any kind, before Adam. The line itself names men. Angels are not men, and the cherub’s iniquity is not “death passed upon all men.”

The objection is fair to raise. If death enters by Adam and passes upon all men, can a world already have been judged before him? Read the limit in the verse. Death passed upon all men, “for that all have sinned.” The reason clause is human sin, in the line of the one man by whom sin entered. One reading: the verse does not say death passed upon angels, and it does not say the cherub could not have fallen in a world that was not Adam’s. It fixes the entrance of sin and death for mankind. That is why the same verse was used above to keep the cherub’s iniquity off Adam’s timeline, not to deny the verse.

Cross-referenceRomans 5:12 KJV

“Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:”

12Eight Reasons, Side by Side

GENESIS 1:1–3 · GENESIS 3:15

The reading stands or falls by the lines, not by a mood. Eight connections are on the table. The last one is not a separate proof text so much as what the dominion lines explain: the hostility toward mankind, once Adam is given the dominion that had been lost.

Key points · the eight

One reading

Seven lines of evidence, then the hostility

What this shows: The case is a chain: Genesis 1:2 after 1:1; judgment language in Jeremiah 4:23; Isaiah 45:18; Job 38:7; Ezekiel 28:13–15 with Isaiah 14:13–14; the overflow in 2 Peter 3:5–6; and “replenish” in Genesis 1:28.

Why it matters: The eighth connection is the enmity. Adam is given the dominion the cherub lost, and the serpent meets the woman already hostile (Genesis 3:1; Genesis 3:15).

Walk them once more, in order.

1. “And” opens Genesis 1:2. Verse 2 follows verse 1. Some read verses 1–3 as one continuous event.

2. “Without form, and void” is the phrase Jeremiah sees under fierce anger (Jeremiah 4:23–26). That reading of verse 2 is a ruin, which does not leave room for a long watery creation process, and it does not smuggle in millions of years.

3. God created the earth not in vain, and formed it to be inhabited (Isaiah 45:18). Verse 2 is not that condition.

4. The morning stars sang, and all the sons of God shouted, when the foundations were laid (Job 38:4–7).

5. The anointed cherub was in Eden, perfect until iniquity (Ezekiel 28:12–15). Lucifer’s throne had to ascend to reach the stars and the clouds (Isaiah 14:12–14). Sin enters Adam’s world by one man (Romans 5:12), so that fall is not inside Adam’s week.

6. The world that then was perished, overflowed with water, and the heavens of old are named with it, from the beginning of the creation (2 Peter 3:4–7). Noah and the ark are not in the passage. Revelation 21:1 likewise has a heaven passing, not the earth only. Cross-check: what Peter still looks for is new heavens and a new earth (2 Peter 3:13).

7. “Fill” is the word for the fish and for Israel’s first filling of Egypt. “Replenish” is the word for Adam and for Noah (Genesis 1:22; 1:28; Genesis 9:1; Exodus 1:7). Webster’s 1828 intransitive sense is “to recover former fullness.”

8. The connection: Adam is given dominion over all the earth (Genesis 1:26; 1:28), the dominion a king and anointed cherub had held and defiled (Ezekiel 28:12; 28:14; 28:18). By Genesis 3:1 the serpent is already there, already subtil, already against the word God had spoken. Genesis 3:15 names the enmity. The hostility toward mankind fits a ruler who lost the earth to the man God put in his place. Christ remains the one by whom all of it, visible and invisible, was made (Colossians 1:16–17).

“And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.”

ScriptureGenesis 3:1 KJV

“Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made. And he said unto the woman, Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?”

Cross-referenceColossians 1:16–17 KJV

“For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him: And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.”
