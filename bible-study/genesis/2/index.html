Genesis 2 — Rest, Dust & the Word We Speak

The seventh day, the making of man and woman, and the deeper reading of salvation underneath them.

01The Seventh Day & Rest

GENESIS 2:1–3

God finishes His work and, on the seventh day, stops. "Rest" here does not mean He grew tired — in older English it carries the sense of ceasing. He blessed this day and made it holy, setting the number seven apart as a marker that runs through the whole Bible.

02Formed from Dust

GENESIS 2:7

Man is formed "of the dust of the ground." God could have used gold or precious stone; He chose dust — the lowly thing that is stepped on and overlooked, yet the very thing from which all life grows. The lesson is humility: our true image apart from God is dust, and from that low place thankfulness and life spring up.

03Bone of My Bones

GENESIS 2:21–24

The woman is not formed from the ground as the man was, but taken from his side while he sleeps. Adam's response — "this is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh" — names a deep kinship: the two are made of one, and in marriage become "one flesh."

04"So Shall It Be"

GENESIS 2:19–20 · MATTHEW 12:37

God brings the animals to the man to name them, and "whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof." Whatever he spoke, it stood. This is the power of the tongue: by our words we are justified or condemned. What we call ourselves tends to become what we live as.

05The Word "But"

1 CORINTHIANS 6:9–11 · ROMANS 3:19–20 · JAMES 2:10

A list of the unrighteous ends with one hinge word — "but" — and everything before it is overturned: "such were some of you, but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified." Each verb is past tense: already done. The law was never the thing that cleans us; it exposes sin and drives us to grace.
