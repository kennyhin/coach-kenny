Coach Kenny · Bible Study · Key points

What God started in Genesis, Jesus brings back, and Revelation finishes.

Genesis 1 and 2 is the first page of the Bible. Revelation is the last page. Read them side by side and the same things come back: light, water, the Word, rest. Jesus is the bridge in the middle.

Text says what the verse itself says. One reading a way of reading it that goes past the words, so weigh it yourself. Just a theory an idea offered as a guess, not a teaching.

1Light

Genesis Genesis 1:3 → Jesus John 1:4-9 → Revelation Revelation 21:23

One reading

Light came before the sun, and the light is God

What this shows: The light of day one existed before the sun and comes from God (Genesis 1:3–5; John 1:4–9).

Why it matters: The end of the Bible matches: no need of the sun, for God gives the light (Revelation 21:23; Revelation 22:5).

Genesis 1:3-5, John 1:4-9, Revelation 21:23, 22:5

The sun does not show up until day four, so the light of day one is not sunlight. John 1 says "in him was life; and the life was the light of men," and Revelation says there is "no need of the sun" because God gives them light. Takeaway: keep it simple. God is the light.

One reading

God never calls the darkness good, or says He created it

What this shows: God called the light good but never said He created the darkness or called it good (Genesis 1:2–4).

Why it matters: Darkness is a sign of chaos, not creation; at the end there is no night, so God does not need evil to exist (Revelation 22:5).

Genesis 1:2-5

Light is the first thing God makes and He calls it good. The darkness is simply already there, and the Bible never says God made it or that it was good. It is something God separates out. That fits the way Revelation ends, with no night at all.

One reading

The day 4 lights are different from the day 1 light

What this shows: God divided light from darkness on day one; the sun, moon and stars take that job on day four (Genesis 1:4; Genesis 1:14–18).

Why it matters: The first light was God, different from the lights perceived today (Revelation 21:23).

Genesis 1:14-19

On day four God sets lights in the firmament "to divide the day from the night" and "for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years." But day one already had day and night. So the day-one light reads as a different light from the sun and moon, and now the sun, moon and stars take over the job.

Text says

Signs, seasons, and no date-setting

What this shows: The lights are for signs, seasons, days and years (Genesis 1:14; Matthew 16:2–3).

Why it matters: Human calendars are only partly accurate, so date-setting fails (Acts 1:7).

Genesis 1:14, Matthew 16:2-3, Acts 1:7

The lights are for "signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years." Jesus said people read the sky but miss "the signs of the times." Man's calendars drift, so watch the seasons God gives, but don't set dates.

“And he said unto them, It is not for you to know the times or the seasons, which the Father hath put in his own power.”Acts 1:7 (KJV)

2Night

Genesis Genesis 1:5 → Jesus John 8:12 → Revelation Revelation 21:25, 22:5

Text says

Day and Night match the end of the Bible

What this shows: God called the light Day and the darkness Night (Genesis 1:5).

Why it matters: Revelation matches it: "there shall be no night there," and God gives the light (Revelation 21:25; Revelation 22:5).

Genesis 1:5, Revelation 21:25, 22:5

God called the light Day and the darkness Night. In Revelation, the heavenly city has no night, because God is the light. The first page and the last pages use the same words, and it is neat how the Bible lines up with itself like that.

One reading

Evening and morning: real days

What this shows: Each day is "evening and morning," a 24-hour day (Genesis 1:5; Genesis 1:8).

Why it matters: It answers theistic evolution's claim that each day is a long age (Genesis 1:5).

Genesis 1:5, 1:8

The day is described as "the evening and the morning." The text itself says how God counts a day, so it is a normal day, not a long age. That is why the sun and moon are not needed to keep the clock in the first days.

One reading

Plants before the sun is no problem for God

What this shows: Plants came a day before the sun (Genesis 1:11–19).

Why it matters: God can keep plants alive by His own power, and the order runs opposite to evolutionary teaching (Genesis 1:12).

Genesis 1:11-19

Plants are made on day three and the sun on day four. That is the opposite of the expected order. The point is that God was not bound by the order people assume it has to go. He made it and He sustained it.

3Water

Genesis Genesis 1:6-10, 1:20 → Jesus John 3:5, 7:38 → Revelation Revelation 4:6, 22:1

One reading

The firmament: a vault of space

What this shows: The firmament is a beaten expanse, a vault: mostly open space, with the sun, moon and stars set in it (Genesis 1:6–8; Genesis 1:14–17).

Why it matters: Scripture explains its own word and removes the supposed contradiction (Genesis 1:8).

Genesis 1:6-8, 1:14-17

God puts a firmament in the middle of the waters, with waters above and waters below. He calls it "Heaven." The sun, moon and stars are set in it (verse 17), so the firmament is a huge open space. "Firmament" means a stretched-out, beaten-out expanse, like a vault. A vault is mostly empty room under a solid roof. That one picture makes the whole thing easier to see.

One reading

The waters above are the sea of glass

What this shows: One reading: the waters above the firmament are the sea of glass at the floor of heaven (Genesis 1:7; Revelation 4:6).

Why it matters: Genesis 1 answers where heaven's sea of glass came from (Revelation 4:6).

Genesis 1:7-10, Revelation 4:6

If heaven sits above the universe, the waters above the firmament sit at the floor of heaven. Revelation 4:6 describes, in front of God's throne, "a sea of glass like unto crystal." One reading: it is the same water, and the waters below became the seas of verse 10. It is a neat match.

One reading

More than one heaven

What this shows: The firmament is called Heaven, separate from the heaven of verse 1; Scripture names a third heaven (Genesis 1:1; Genesis 1:8; 2 Corinthians 12:2).

Why it matters: It identifies the second heaven as outer space, where the sun, moon and stars are set (Genesis 1:17).

Genesis 1:1, 1:8, 2 Corinthians 12:2

Verse 1 says God created "the heaven." In verse 8 God "called the firmament Heaven." The Bible talks about three heavens (Paul was caught up to the third in 2 Corinthians 12), so one reading is the sky and space below, and God's dwelling above.

Text says

The waters gather so the land can appear

What this shows: The dry land was under the waters and appeared when they gathered (Genesis 1:9–10).

Why it matters: The waters under the heaven became today's seas (Genesis 1:10).

Genesis 1:9-10

On day three God says let the waters gather "unto one place, and let the dry land appear." The land was already there, under the water, and it comes up. The gathered waters are called seas, which is the same word used for the waters below the firmament.

One reading

Life from the water, and born of water

What this shows: One reading: life from water pictures the physical birth, and Jesus adds a second, spiritual birth (Genesis 1:20; John 3:3–6).

Why it matters: "Born of water" is not water baptism; a person must be born again (John 3:6).

Genesis 1:20-23, John 3:3-6

On day five God says let the waters bring forth moving creatures that have life. Then Jesus tells Nicodemus a man must be born of water and of the Spirit (John 3). One way to read it: "born of water" is the natural birth (when a woman's water breaks) and "born of the Spirit" is the second, spiritual birth. So the first life comes out of water, and so does the first birth.

One reading

Abundant water, rivers of living water

What this shows: One reading: abundant water pictures "rivers of living water," the Spirit (Genesis 1:20; John 7:38–39).

Why it matters: God had the believer's spiritual life in mind when He created (John 7:39).

Genesis 1:20, John 7:37-39

The waters bring forth life "abundantly." John 7:38 says out of the believer will flow "rivers of living water," and verse 39 says that is the Holy Spirit. One reading: God was thinking ahead to what the Spirit would do in the believer.

One reading

Two meanings of "earth"

What this shows: "Earth" means the whole planet in verse 1 and the dry land in verse 10 (Genesis 1:1; Genesis 1:10; Job 26:7; 1 Samuel 2:8).

Why it matters: Genesis 1 gives the distinction, so the verses fit together (Genesis 1:10).

Genesis 1:1, 1:10, Job 26:7, 1 Samuel 2:8

In verse 1 "the earth" is the whole world. In verse 10 God calls the dry land "Earth." Job says the world hangs on nothing, and Hannah says the land sits on the LORD's pillars. Ask which earth a verse means and the two fit together.

“He stretcheth out the north over the empty place, and hangeth the earth upon nothing.”Job 26:7 (KJV)

Just a theory

Four lifeless days, then life (a theory)

What this shows: A theory only: four lifeless days may picture about 4,000 years before Christ came to give life (2 Peter 3:8; Galatians 4:4).

Why it matters: Everlasting life comes through Christ (John 3:16).

2 Peter 3:8, Galatians 4:4, John 3:16

If a day is with the Lord as a thousand years, days one to four have no creature and life comes on day five. That could picture about 4,000 years from Adam to Christ, who brings everlasting life. It's only a theory, and the creation days are still real days.

“…one day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day.”2 Peter 3:8 (KJV)

4The Word

Genesis Genesis 1:3 → Jesus John 1:1-3 → Revelation Revelation 19:13

One reading

The first thing the Bible says is God

What this shows: "In the beginning God": God is set next to the beginning (Genesis 1:1).

Genesis 1:1

The very first word after "In the beginning" is "God." Nothing comes before Him. That is how the whole Bible opens.

One reading

The Word that spoke creation is the Word of John 1

What this shows: God spoke and light came; the Word made all things (Genesis 1:3; John 1:1–3).

Why it matters: Creation came by the voice of God, not by impersonal forces (Psalm 33:6).

Genesis 1:3, John 1:1-3

God said "Let there be light," and there was light. John 1:1-3 says who the Word is: everything was made by Him. So when God speaks in Genesis 1, the Word is at work. Creation is made by speaking.

One reading

"Kind" is God's plain word

What this shows: "Kind" is God's plain word; scientists do not use it (Genesis 1:11–25).

Why it matters: Keep to God's wording instead of moving the question into scientific terms (1 Timothy 6:20).

Genesis 1:11-25

God uses everyday words. A child can tell a horse from a cat, and "kind" is the word God picked. Nobody needs a science textbook to follow the story. Takeaway: stay with God's wording and do not chase it into lab terms.

Text says

God numbers and names every star

What this shows: "He made the stars also"; God numbers them and names them all (Genesis 1:16; Psalm 147:4).

Why it matters: God is far greater and more trustworthy than men who struggle to count and name the stars (Psalm 147:5).

Genesis 1:16, Psalm 147:4

"He made the stars also." That is all Genesis says, like it is nothing. Psalm 147:4 says God "telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names." God already knows each one by name.

Just a theory

Adam named the animals in under a day (a theory)

What this shows: A theory only: Adam named every creature on day six, before the woman was made (Genesis 2:19–23).

Why it matters: It shows the intellect God gave mankind (Genesis 2:19).

Genesis 1:27, 2:19-23

Everything on day six fits in one day: the animals, then Adam, then he names every animal, then Eve. If that is right, it shows how sharp Adam's mind was. This one is only a theory, not a fact.

Text says

Great whales, and Jonah

What this shows: Genesis names "great whales"; Jesus calls Jonah's great fish "the whale" (Genesis 1:21; Jonah 1:17; Matthew 12:40).

Why it matters: God may name His creation as He wills; the words are to be left alone (Psalm 12:6).

Genesis 1:21, Jonah 1:17, Matthew 12:40

Genesis names "great whales" first among the sea creatures. Jonah calls it "a great fish," and Jesus calls it "the whale." God names His creatures in plain words, and Jesus made Jonah the sign of His own resurrection.

“For as Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale’s belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth.”Matthew 12:40 (KJV)

5"Us"

Genesis Genesis 1:26 → Jesus Matthew 28:19 → Revelation Revelation 22:1, 22:17

One reading

The Spirit moves, then God speaks

What this shows: The Spirit (breath) moved upon the waters, then God spoke (Genesis 1:2–3).

Why it matters: Spirit means life: after death and destruction, the Spirit moves before creation begins (Ecclesiastes 12:7).

Genesis 1:2-3

In verse 2 "the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters." Spirit also means breath. Then in verse 3 God speaks. The picture: the Spirit moves first, then the voice of God, and creation answers.

One reading

"Let us make man": the Trinity on page one

What this shows: God says "let us make man in our image," then man is made in "his own image": one God, more than one Person (Genesis 1:26–27).

Why it matters: It cannot be angels, since only God creates; the Old Testament's first chapter shows the Trinity (Genesis 1:27).

Genesis 1:26-27

God says "Let us make man in our image" and then verse 27 says God created man "in his own image." One God speaking as "us." It cannot be angels, because angels do not make anyone. So this is a first look at the Trinity, right in the first chapter.

One reading

Man rules the earth, not the heavens

What this shows: Dominion is over the earth, not the heavens (Genesis 1:26; Genesis 11:3–4).

Why it matters: Reaching into heaven is the spirit of Babel and Lucifer, which God does not approve (Isaiah 14:12–14).

Genesis 1:26, Genesis 11:3-4, Isaiah 14:12-14

Dominion is given "over the fish of the sea... the fowl of the air... and over all the earth." It does not say over the sun, moon and stars. Babel (a tower "whose top may reach unto heaven") and Isaiah 14 ("I will ascend into heaven") are both about reaching up. One reading: man's job is the earth, and the heavens stay God's.

One reading

Lucifer ruled the earth before man

What this shows: One reading: Lucifer ruled the earth before man and fell before the six days (Isaiah 14:12–14; Ezekiel 28:13–15).

Why it matters: It explains why the serpent is already present and hostile to man (Genesis 3:1).

Genesis 1:28, Isaiah 14:12-14, Ezekiel 28:13-15, Genesis 3:1

Lucifer had a throne below the stars and was "in Eden." Then God gives man dominion over every living thing on the earth. His fall can't fit inside six "very good" days, and the serpent is already there in Genesis 3. One reading puts the fall before the six days.

“…I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God…”Isaiah 14:13 (KJV)

6After his kind: one Designer

Genesis Genesis 1:11-25 → Jesus Colossians 1:16 → Revelation Revelation 4:11

One reading

Five building blocks in one verse

What this shows: Genesis 1:1 contains time (beginning), motion and energy (created), space (heaven) and matter (earth) (Genesis 1:1).

Genesis 1:1

One short verse holds the basics of the physical world: time ("beginning"), force or energy ("created"), space ("heaven") and matter ("earth"). It works as a memory picture for how much is packed into the first line.

One reading

One verse answers the big worldviews

What this shows: Genesis 1:1 rules out polytheism, atheism, evolution, deism and an eternal universe (Genesis 1:1).

Why it matters: God is the origin of the universe; it did not always exist (Isaiah 45:18).

Genesis 1:1

Verse 1 answers a lot in one line. God is one (not many gods). God exists. The heaven and the earth were made, not self-made. And the universe had a start, so it is not eternal and it is not God.

Text says

Plants and seed in themselves

What this shows: Each plant has "seed in itself" and reproduces after its kind (Genesis 1:11–12).

Genesis 1:11-13

God says let the earth bring forth grass, herbs and fruit trees. Each one has "seed in itself." So reproduction is built in from the start. Gardeners still use that seed today to grow the same fruit.

One reading

"After his kind" is the key phrase

What this shows: "After his kind" repeats again and again: no common ancestry outside a kind (Genesis 1:11–12; Genesis 1:21; Genesis 1:24–25).

Why it matters: The phrase is repeated so it cannot be missed, and it stands against evolution (Genesis 1:21).

Genesis 1:11-12, 21, 24-25

"After his kind" shows up again and again. Plants produce plants, birds produce birds, cattle produce cattle. A dog can have different breeds of dogs, but a dog does not produce a cat. The Bible says it so many times, it feels like it is on purpose.

One reading

A common designer, not a common ancestor

What this shows: Similar traits across living things fit a common designer, not a common ancestor (Genesis 1:21–25).

Why it matters: Shared design should point to creation and to God (Romans 1:20).

Genesis 1:11-12, 21-25

People point to how alike living things are, even in their DNA, and say it proves one shared ancestor. But that match is what you get from one Designer. Artists, inventors and builders all leave their style in their work. Same style does not mean one painting gave birth to another.

Text says

Insects and animals made the same day

What this shows: Insects, cattle and beasts were made on the same day (Genesis 1:24–25).

Why it matters: Insects did not come billions of years before other animals (Genesis 1:25).

Genesis 1:24-25

On day six the earth brings forth cattle, creeping things and beasts, each "after his kind." Bugs and pets and wild animals all show up on the same day. They are not separated by long ages.

One reading

Be fruitful: plants, animals and man share the same words

What this shows: The same words, seed, fruit and "be fruitful," run from plants to animals to man (Genesis 1:11; Genesis 1:22; Genesis 1:28).

Why it matters: The connection is one common Designer, not one patent giving birth to another (Genesis 1:28).

Genesis 1:11, 22, 28

God tells the plants to yield seed and fruit, the fish and birds to be fruitful and multiply, and then man "be fruitful, and multiply." Same words all the way up. That link is not because one came from another. It is one Designer using one pattern for all life.

One reading

Male and female is a puzzle for evolution

What this shows: Male and female must fit together for reproduction from the start (Genesis 1:27).

Why it matters: It points to a mind behind creation (Genesis 1:27).

Genesis 1:27

"Male and female created he them." Why does life so often come in two kinds that fit together? Reproduction is needed for survival from the start, so how could the two parts slowly evolve and still match? One reading: it looks like a mind put it together on purpose.

One reading

Fill, then replenish

What this shows: "Fill" is a first filling; "replenish" is filling again (Genesis 1:22; Genesis 1:28; Genesis 9:1; Exodus 1:7).

Why it matters: It points to a former world before Adam (Genesis 1:28).

Genesis 1:22, 1:28, 9:1, Exodus 1:7

Fish and birds "fill" the waters. Adam is told to "replenish" the earth. So is Noah after the flood, while Israel "filled" Egypt the first time. One reading: replenish means to fill again, a hint of an earlier world.

“And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth.”Genesis 9:1 (KJV)

7Rest and peace

Genesis Genesis 2:1-3, 1:29-30 → Jesus Matthew 11:28 → Revelation Revelation 21:4, Isaiah 11:6-9

Text says

The first diet was plants, for everyone

What this shows: Man and beast were first given plants for food (Genesis 1:29–30).

Why it matters: The future kingdom restores that peace (Isaiah 11:6–7).

Genesis 1:29-30, Isaiah 11:6-7

In Genesis 1:29-30 God gives every herb and tree fruit "for meat" (food), and to every beast and bird "every green herb." So at the start nobody ate anyone. Isaiah 11 says the wolf shall dwell with the lamb and the lion shall eat straw like the ox, and one reading sees that as paradise coming back.

One reading

"Very good", even in a fallen world

What this shows: Everything God made was "very good" (Genesis 1:31).

Why it matters: The world's beauty remains God's handiwork even after the fall and the flood (Genesis 13:10).

Genesis 1:31

Day six ends with God seeing everything He made, and it was "very good." Look around: even now, after the fall and Noah's flood, there are still so many beautiful places. That is the handiwork of God.

One reading

The seventh day: blessed, holy and a pattern

What this shows: God blessed and sanctified the seventh day, and seven runs through Scripture (Genesis 2:1–3; Leviticus 25:8–10).

Why it matters: The pattern points toward the millennium, the whole earth at rest like Eden, and gives clues to the times (1 Thessalonians 5:1–2).

Genesis 2:1-3, Leviticus (Jubilee, ch. 25†), 1 Thessalonians 5

God did not have to add a seventh day. He could have started over with day one. He blessed it and made it holy, and the Bible keeps coming back to seven (Sabbath, seven sevens leading to Jubilee, the millennium as a rest). That is not an accident.

One reading

"Rest" means to cease, not to get tired

What this shows: "Rested" means ceased; God was not tired (Genesis 2:2).

Why it matters: God is omnipotent; He made man with a breath (Genesis 2:7).

Genesis 2:1-3, Genesis 2:7

Genesis 2:2 says God "rested from all his work." In old English, rest means to cease, to stop. God was not worn out. In Genesis 2:7 all He did was breathe and man became a living soul. Resting means He finished and stopped.

One reading

"All the host of them"

What this shows: One reading: "host" means stars and angels, so Genesis 2:1 may sum up all creation (Genesis 2:1; Job 38:7).

Genesis 2:1, Nehemiah 9:6, Job 38:7

"Host" in Scripture means the stars and also the angels. Job puts the morning stars and the sons of God together. One reading: Genesis 2:1 may sum up everything from Genesis 1:1, not only the six days.

“…thou hast made heaven, the heaven of heavens, with all their host…”Nehemiah 9:6 (KJV)
