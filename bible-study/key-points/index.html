Key Points & Aha Moments

These are the things that jumped out at me while studying Genesis 1 and 2, in plain words, with the verses I used. Below them is a map of how it all connects.

The big idea in three lines

  1. God speaks and it happens. The Spirit moves, the Word speaks, and light, land, stars and life answer.
  2. The Bible lines up with itself. Day and Night in Genesis 1 match Revelation 22, and the first diet in Genesis 1 matches Isaiah 11.
  3. One Designer, one pattern. Seed in itself, after his kind, be fruitful: the same words run from plants to people.

How to read the labels

Text says what the verse itself says. One reading a way of reading it that goes past the words, so weigh it yourself. Just a theory an idea offered as a guess, not a teaching. References marked † are ones I added because the chapter was not named.

The themes, in plain words

Father, Word and Spirit

The Spirit moves on the waters, the Word speaks, and God says "us." All three show up in the first chapter.

Spirit moves on the waters → The Word spoke it (John 1) → Let us make man → Light into a dark heart

Light and darkness

Day-one light, God as the light, Day and Night, and the day-four lights. It runs from Genesis 1 to Revelation 22.

Light before the sun → Day and Night match Rev. 22 → Day 4 lights, a new light → Light into a dark heart → Evening, then morning

Water, from the deep to new birth

The Spirit over the waters, waters divided, seas gathered, life from water, and being born of water and the Spirit.

Spirit moves on the waters → Waters above: sea of glass → Seas gathered, land rises → Life from water, John 3 → The word "but": washed

After his kind: one Designer

Seed in itself, after his kind, be fruitful, and male and female. The same pattern in plants, animals and people.

Verse 1 answers the worldviews → After his kind → One Designer, not one ancestor → Be fruitful, same words → Male and female

Words and names

God speaks, God names the stars, Adam names the animals, and what we call things matters.

The Word spoke it (John 1) → God names the stars → Adam names the animals → "So shall it be": the tongue

The heavens and who rules what

The firmament is called Heaven, there is more than one heaven, and man is given the earth, not the sky.

Firmament: a vault of space → More than one heaven → Day 4 lights, a new light → Man rules earth, not sky

Days, time and the seventh day

Evening and morning, plants before the sun, a quick day six, and a seventh day that is blessed and set apart.

Evening and morning → Plants before the sun → Adam names the animals → Seventh day, set apart → Formed from dust

Paradise: first page, last page

The first diet and the very good world, Isaiah 11 and the rest of the millennium, and Revelation 22 with no night.

Day and Night match Rev. 22 → First diet: plants → Seventh day, set apart

Teaching 1: Genesis 1:1-8

The first verse, the dark deep, light, and the firmament.

1.1One reading

The first thing the Bible says is God

Genesis 1:1

The very first word after "In the beginning" is "God." Nothing comes before Him. That is how the whole Bible opens.

Connects to: Father, Word and Spirit

1.2One reading

Five building blocks in one verse

Genesis 1:1

One short verse holds the basics of the physical world: time ("beginning"), force or energy ("created"), space ("heaven") and matter ("earth"). I like it as a memory picture for how much is packed into the first line.

Connects to: After his kind: one Designer

1.3One reading

One verse answers the big worldviews

Genesis 1:1

Verse 1 answers a lot in one line. God is one (not many gods). God exists. The heaven and the earth were made, not self-made. And the universe had a start, so it is not eternal and it is not God.

Connects to: After his kind: one Designer

1.4One reading

The Spirit moves, then God speaks

Genesis 1:2-3

In verse 2 "the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters." Spirit also means breath. Then in verse 3 God speaks. I like the picture: the Spirit moves first, then the voice of God, and creation answers.

Connects to: Father, Word and Spirit Water, from the deep to new birth

1.5One reading

The Word that spoke creation is the Word of John 1

Genesis 1:3, John 1:1-3

God said "Let there be light," and there was light. John 1:1-3 tells me who the Word is: everything was made by Him. So when God speaks in Genesis 1, I see the Word at work. Creation is made by speaking.

Connects to: Father, Word and Spirit Words and names

1.6One reading

Light came before the sun, and the light is God

Genesis 1:3-5, John 1:4-9, Revelation 21:23, 22:5

The sun does not show up until day four, so the light of day one is not sunlight. John 1 says "in him was life; and the life was the light of men," and Revelation says there is "no need of the sun" because God gives them light. My takeaway: keep it simple. God is the light.

Connects to: Light and darkness Father, Word and Spirit

1.7One reading

God never calls the darkness good, or says He created it

Genesis 1:2-5

Light is the first thing God makes and He calls it good. The darkness is simply already there, and the Bible never says God made it or that it was good. It is something God separates out. That fits the way Revelation ends, with no night at all.

Connects to: Light and darkness

1.8Text says

Day and Night match the end of the Bible

Genesis 1:5, Revelation 21:25, 22:5

God called the light Day and the darkness Night. In Revelation, the heavenly city has no night, because God is the light. The first page and the last pages use the same words, and it is neat how the Bible lines up with itself like that.

Connects to: Light and darkness Paradise: first page, last page

1.9One reading

Evening and morning: real days

Genesis 1:5, 1:8

The day is described as "the evening and the morning." The text itself says how God counts a day, so it is a normal day, not a long age. That is why the sun and moon are not needed to keep the clock in the first days.

Connects to: Days, time and the seventh day

1.10One reading

The firmament: a vault of space

Genesis 1:6-8, 1:14-17

God puts a firmament in the middle of the waters, with waters above and waters below. He calls it "Heaven." The sun, moon and stars are set in it (verse 17), so the firmament is a huge open space. "Firmament" means a stretched-out, beaten-out expanse, like a vault. A vault is mostly empty room under a solid roof. That one picture makes the whole thing easier to see.

Connects to: The heavens and who rules what

1.11One reading

The waters above are the sea of glass

Genesis 1:7-10, Revelation 4:6

If heaven sits above the universe, the waters above the firmament sit at the floor of heaven. Revelation 4:6 describes, in front of God's throne, "a sea of glass like unto crystal." I read it as the same water, and the waters below became the seas of verse 10. It is a neat match.

Connects to: Water, from the deep to new birth The heavens and who rules what

1.12One reading

More than one heaven

Genesis 1:1, 1:8, 2 Corinthians 12:2

Verse 1 says God created "the heaven." In verse 8 God "called the firmament Heaven." The Bible talks about three heavens (Paul was caught up to the third in 2 Corinthians 12), so I read it as the sky and space below, and God's dwelling above.

Connects to: The heavens and who rules what

Teaching 2: Genesis 1:9-25

Land and seas, plants, the lights, and life after its kind.

2.1Text says

The waters gather so the land can appear

Genesis 1:9-10

On day three God says let the waters gather "unto one place, and let the dry land appear." The land was already there, under the water, and it comes up. The gathered waters are called seas, which is the same word used for the waters below the firmament.

Connects to: Water, from the deep to new birth The heavens and who rules what

2.2Text says

Plants and seed in themselves

Genesis 1:11-13

God says let the earth bring forth grass, herbs and fruit trees. Each one has "seed in itself." So reproduction is built in from the start. Gardeners still use that seed today to grow the same fruit.

Connects to: After his kind: one Designer

2.3One reading

"After his kind" is the key phrase

Genesis 1:11-12, 21, 24-25

"After his kind" shows up again and again. Plants produce plants, birds produce birds, cattle produce cattle. A dog can have different breeds of dogs, but a dog does not produce a cat. The Bible says it so many times, it feels like it is on purpose.

Connects to: After his kind: one Designer

2.4One reading

A common designer, not a common ancestor

Genesis 1:11-12, 21-25

People point to how alike living things are, even in their DNA, and say it proves one shared ancestor. The answer in this teaching: that match is what you get from one Designer. Artists, inventors and builders all leave their style in their work. Same style does not mean one painting gave birth to another.

Connects to: After his kind: one Designer

2.5One reading

"Kind" is God's plain word

Genesis 1:11-25

God uses everyday words. A child can tell a horse from a cat, and "kind" is the word God picked. Nobody needs a science textbook to follow the story. The point I took: stay with God's wording and do not chase it into lab terms.

Connects to: After his kind: one Designer

2.6One reading

The day 4 lights are different from the day 1 light

Genesis 1:14-19

On day four God sets lights in the firmament "to divide the day from the night" and "for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years." But day one already had day and night. So I read the day-one light as a different light from the sun and moon, and now the sun, moon and stars take over the job.

Connects to: Light and darkness The heavens and who rules what

2.7Text says

God numbers and names every star

Genesis 1:16, Psalm 147:4

"He made the stars also." That is all Genesis says, like it is nothing. Psalm 147:4 says God "telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names." Scientists need computers just to keep track of names. God already knows each one.

Connects to: Words and names

2.8One reading

Plants before the sun is no problem for God

Genesis 1:11-19

Plants are made on day three and the sun on day four. That is the opposite of what we would expect. The point is that God was not stuck by the order we think it has to go. He made it and He sustained it.

Connects to: Days, time and the seventh day

2.9One reading

Life from the water, and born of water

Genesis 1:20-23, John 3:3-6

On day five God says let the waters bring forth moving creatures that have life. Then Jesus tells Nicodemus a man must be born of water and of the Spirit (John 3). One way to read it: "born of water" is the natural birth (when a woman's water breaks) and "born of the Spirit" is the second, spiritual birth. So the first life comes out of water, and so does the first birth.

Connects to: Water, from the deep to new birth Father, Word and Spirit

2.10One reading

Abundant water, rivers of living water

Genesis 1:20, John 7:37-39

The waters bring forth life "abundantly." John 7:38 says out of the believer will flow "rivers of living water," and verse 39 says that is the Holy Spirit. I read it as God thinking ahead to what the Spirit would do in the believer.

Connects to: Water, from the deep to new birth

2.11Text says

Insects and animals made the same day

Genesis 1:24-25

On day six the earth brings forth cattle, creeping things and beasts, each "after his kind." Bugs and pets and wild animals all show up on the same day. They are not separated by long ages.

Connects to: After his kind: one Designer

Teaching 3: Genesis 1:26 - 2:3

Man in God's image, dominion, the first diet, and the seventh day.

3.1One reading

"Let us make man": the Trinity on page one

Genesis 1:26-27

God says "Let us make man in our image" and then verse 27 says God created man "in his own image." One God speaking as "us." It cannot be angels, because angels do not make anyone. So this is a first look at the Trinity, right in the first chapter.

Connects to: Father, Word and Spirit

3.2One reading

Man rules the earth, not the heavens

Genesis 1:26, Genesis 11:3-4, Isaiah 14:12-14

Dominion is given "over the fish of the sea... the fowl of the air... and over all the earth." It does not say over the sun, moon and stars. Babel (a tower "whose top may reach unto heaven") and Isaiah 14 ("I will ascend into heaven") are both about reaching up. My read: man's job is the earth, and the heavens stay God's.

Connects to: The heavens and who rules what

3.3One reading

Be fruitful: plants, animals and man share the same words

Genesis 1:11, 22, 28

God tells the plants to yield seed and fruit, the fish and birds to be fruitful and multiply, and then man "be fruitful, and multiply." Same words all the way up. That link is not because one came from another. It is one Designer using one pattern for all life.

Connects to: After his kind: one Designer

3.4One reading

Male and female is a puzzle for evolution

Genesis 1:27

"Male and female created he them." Why does life so often come in two kinds that fit together? Reproduction is needed for survival from the start, so how could the two parts slowly evolve and still match? To me it looks like a mind put it together on purpose.

Connects to: After his kind: one Designer

3.5Text says

The first diet was plants, for everyone

Genesis 1:29-30, Isaiah 11:6-7

In Genesis 1:29-30 God gives every herb and tree fruit "for meat" (food), and to every beast and bird "every green herb." So at the start nobody ate anyone. Isaiah 11 says the wolf shall dwell with the lamb and the lion shall eat straw like the ox, and I read that as paradise coming back.

Connects to: Paradise: first page, last page

3.6One reading

"Very good", even in a fallen world

Genesis 1:31

Day six ends with God seeing everything He made, and it was "very good." Look around: even now, after the fall and Noah's flood, there are still so many beautiful places. That is the handiwork of God.

Connects to: Paradise: first page, last page

3.7Just a theory

Adam named the animals in under a day (a theory)

Genesis 1:27, 2:19-23

Everything on day six fits in one day: the animals, then Adam, then he names every animal, then Eve. If that is right, it shows how sharp Adam's mind was, before any scientist built a classification system. This one is only a theory, not a fact.

Connects to: Words and names Days, time and the seventh day

3.8One reading

The seventh day: blessed, holy and a pattern

Genesis 2:1-3, Leviticus (Jubilee, ch. 25†), 1 Thessalonians 5

God did not have to add a seventh day. He could have started over with day one. He blessed it and made it holy, and the Bible keeps coming back to seven (Sabbath, seven sevens leading to Jubilee, the millennium as a rest). That is not an accident.

Connects to: Days, time and the seventh day Paradise: first page, last page

3.9One reading

"Rest" means to cease, not to get tired

Genesis 2:1-3, Genesis 2:7

Genesis 2:2 says God "rested from all his work." In old English, rest means to cease, to stop. God was not worn out. In Genesis 2:7 all He did was breathe and man became a living soul. Resting means He finished and stopped.

Connects to: Days, time and the seventh day
