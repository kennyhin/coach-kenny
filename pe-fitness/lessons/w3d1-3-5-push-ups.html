Right-Angle Push-ups

Today we will

Lesson Objective

Students will be able to perform right-angle push-ups with a partner counting only good reps (body straight, arms bending to about a right angle, full push-up), and name this as one of the two upper-body choices.

Unit Objective

Students will demonstrate safe form and honest effort on the six Presidential Fitness Test events and complete official testing in all three categories (core, cardio, and upper body) so they can earn a Presidential Award, a National Award, or a Certificate of Participation.

Unit Essential Question

How do I train, choose one event in each category, and show my best honest effort on the Presidential Fitness Test?

Bell Ringer/Warm-up

Enter and sit on squad spots. Recap: "We have practiced core and cardio. This week is upper body." Dynamic warm-up (about 5 minutes): arm circles, scapular squeezes, wall push-ups or kneeling push-ups, and a short plank (10-15 seconds). Freeze on the attention signal.

Direct Instruction (TW) Teacher

Upper-body category: pick right-angle push-ups or pull-ups. Today is right-angle push-ups.

School form rule (the official PDF does not publish a cadence or every form detail — use this the same way for everyone): start in a straight-body push-up position, hands under the shoulders. Lower until the elbows are about a right angle (about 90 degrees). Push back up to straight arms. That is one rep. Partner counts only reps that keep a straight body and reach that depth and a full push-up. Hips should not sag or pike. No resting on the floor between reps during a counted set; if they rest, the set ends (school rule for counted practice and for testing).

Practice modifications (practice only): wall push-ups or knee push-ups to learn the arm bend. Official testing later should use the full right-angle push-up unless a documented medical plan says otherwise.

Vocabulary: right-angle, rep, push-up, upper body, straight body.

Official right-angle push-ups (reps, at least) and pull-ups (reps, at least) for common 3-5 ages. Look up other ages in the Benchmarks page. Age 5 has no official number.

Boys — Push-ups Presidential / National: age 8 = 17 / 9; 9 = 18 / 12; 10 = 22 / 14; 11 = 27 / 15.

Boys — Pull-ups Presidential / National: age 8 = 5 / 1; 9 = 5 / 2; 10 = 6 / 2; 11 = 6 / 2.

Girls — Push-ups Presidential / National: age 8 = 17 / 9; 9 = 18 / 12; 10 = 20 / 13; 11 = 20 / 13.

Girls — Pull-ups Presidential / National: age 8 = 2 / 1; 9 = 2 / 1; 10 = 3 / 1; 11 = 3 / 1.

Guided Practice (SW) Students

Partners in rows. One works, one watches from the side (best view of the elbow angle), then switch.

  1. Round 1 — Form (4 min): 5 slow push-ups, or knee/wall versions, with the partner saying "right angle" at the bottom.
  2. Round 2 — Short sets (6 min): 2 sets of as many good reps as the student can do with control, rest, switch.
  3. Round 3 — Practice test set (8 min): one continuous set of right-angle push-ups. Partner counts good reps only and may record a practice number. Switch. A second set is optional if time remains.

Coach walks the rows and checks depth and straight bodies. Reset a student who is bouncing or only bending a little.

Cool-down

Standing chest and shoulder stretches (2-3 min).

Kagan/SLAMified — RoundRobin

Groups of 3-4. Each student in turn says one push-up cue (right angle, straight body, full push-up, eyes down, breathe). Two trips around the group.

Exit Ticket

Show-me: from a standing position, bend your arms to show a right angle. Thumbs on "I can explain a good rep to my partner." Coach notes shallow reps for 1:1 next class.

Reminders

Push-up scores are "at least." Example: a 10-year-old boy's Presidential target is 22 (National 14). A 10-year-old girl's Presidential target is 20 (National 13).

No official cadence is published — do not require a special rhythm unless the school later adopts one and uses it for everyone.

Safety: wrists and shoulders. Come down if it hurts. Keep faces off the floor.

Nevada State Standards

  • 1.5.1 (Grades 3-5) — Utilize locomotor and nonlocomotor movements in physical activities.
  • 1.5.4 (Grades 3-5) — Demonstrate safe practices while participating in physical activities.
  • 2.5.3 (Grades 3-5) — Explain the importance of safety rules and procedures for self and others in physical activities.
  • 3.5.1 (Grades 3-5) — Participate in a variety of moderate to vigorous physical activities.
