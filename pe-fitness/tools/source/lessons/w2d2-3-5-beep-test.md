Title: Presidential Fitness Test — 20-Meter Beep Test
Grade band: Grades 3-5
When: Week 2 Day 2

Unit Objective: Students will demonstrate safe form and honest effort on the six Presidential Fitness Test events and complete official testing in all three categories (core, cardio, and upper body) so they can earn a Presidential Award, a National Award, or a Certificate of Participation.

Unit Essential Question: How do I train, choose one event in each category, and show my best honest effort on the Presidential Fitness Test?

Lesson Objective: Students will be able to run 20-meter shuttles, reach the line before each beep (or whistle), and have a partner tally laps, stopping after two missed lines under the school rule, so they understand the second cardio choice.

Equipment: Measuring tape or a pre-measured 20-meter distance, cones or tape for two end lines, beep-test audio and speaker if available (whistle-and-timer backup), extra cones for lanes, partner tally cards or paper, water, whistle.

Bell Ringer/Warm-up:
Enter and sit on squad spots. Recap: "Yesterday was the mile. Today is the other cardio choice — the 20-meter beep test, also called the PACER." Dynamic warm-up (about 5 minutes): jog, high knees, side shuffles, easy 20-meter down-and-backs at a slow jog. Freeze on the attention signal.

Direct Instruction (TW):
Show the two lines, 20 meters apart. Students run to the opposite line and get there before the beep. At the line they turn and wait for the next beep, then run back. Each run from one line to the other is one lap.

The official PDF does not spell out when a runner is out, so use this school rule for every student: a miss is when you do not reach the line before the beep. After two misses, you stop. The partner writes down the number of laps completed (the last good lap before you stopped). That is practice today, not the official test.

If you do not have official beep-test audio, use a whistle and a simple increasing rhythm (start slower, slightly quicker over time). Do not invent official stage numbers. The official score is laps.

Lanes: several students can run at once if each has a clear path. Partners stand off the course and tally. No blocking, no cutting across lanes, no diving at the line.

Vocabulary: 20 meters, beep test, PACER, lap, miss, turn.

Official 20m beep-test targets (laps, at least) for common 3-5 ages. Look up other ages in benchmarks.md. Age 5 has no official number.
Boys Presidential / National: age 8 = 39 / 19; 9 = 47 / 23; 10 = 55 / 26; 11 = 60 / 31.
Girls Presidential / National: age 8 = 29 / 15; 9 = 35 / 18; 10 = 41 / 21; 11 = 46 / 24.

Guided Practice (SW):
Partners. One runs, one tallies, then switch. Coach starts the beeps or whistle.

Round 1 — Walk-through (4 min): walk/jog 4-6 shuttles with a slow whistle so everyone learns "arrive, turn, wait, go."

Round 2 — Short run (6 min): a short beep or whistle set. Stop after two misses, or after a short cap if you want everyone to stay fresh. Switch partners.

Round 3 — Longer practice (8-10 min): a fuller practice trial. Partner counts laps and records a practice number. Switch. If the class is large, run two waves (half run, half tally).

Cool-down:
Walk the long way around the gym or field. Calf, hip, and hamstring stretches (3 min).

Kagan/SLAMified — Mix-Pair-Share:
Students walk the perimeter, mix, and pair on the signal. Round 1: "Tell your partner how a lap is counted." Round 2: "What is our two-miss school rule?" Two rounds.

Exit Ticket:
Short answer at line-up: "How wide is the course?" (20 meters) or "When do you stop?" (two misses). Coach notes students who turned short of the line for a 1:1 next class.

Reminders:
Measure 20 meters carefully and mark both lines so they cannot drift. Use the same school miss rule on practice test day and official testing day.
Example: a 10-year-old boy's Presidential target is 55 laps (National 26). A 10-year-old girl's Presidential target is 41 (National 21).
Safety: stop for dizziness or pain. Give space at the turn. Use a flat, dry surface.
Students will later pick either the mile or the beep test for their official cardio score — both are not required.
