Title: Presidential Fitness Test — One-Mile Run
Grade band: Grades 3-5
When: Week 2 Day 1

Unit Objective: Students will demonstrate safe form and honest effort on the six Presidential Fitness Test events and complete official testing in all three categories (core, cardio, and upper body) so they can earn a Presidential Award, a National Award, or a Certificate of Participation.

Unit Essential Question: How do I train, choose one event in each category, and show my best honest effort on the Presidential Fitness Test?

Lesson Objective: Students will be able to warm up safely, use a talk-test jog (say a short sentence while running), and complete a timed practice on the measured mile course — or a clear set of laps toward one mile — while staying on the path and finishing with control.

Equipment: Measured mile course and cones, stopwatch, whistle, water, shaded rest option if outdoors, coach clipboard if recording practice laps or times. Confirm how many laps equal one mile before class starts.

Bell Ringer/Warm-up:
Enter and sit on squad spots or the designated outdoor line. Dynamic warm-up (about 5 minutes): high knees, butt kicks, walking lunges or knee lifts, arm circles, and one easy jog/walk lap of the course. Freeze on the attention signal.

Direct Instruction (TW):
Today is one of the two cardio choices: the one-mile run. The other cardio choice (the 20m beep test) is next class. For the award, a student will pick one.

Pacing, not a first-lap sprint: model three paces — (1) sprint (too fast for a mile), (2) talk-test jog (you can say a short sentence), (3) recovery walk. Students should use the talk-test jog for most of the run. Walking recoveries are allowed during practice as long as they keep moving forward.

Show the course: start line, running direction (same way for everyone), how many laps equal one mile, and where to finish. Prior SLAM PE plans have used 10 laps for a mile — confirm the course is measured and post the lap count where students can see it.

The mile is the only event where a faster time is better ("at most"). Record minutes:seconds. Form cues: eyes forward, relaxed shoulders, elbows bent, soft landings, steady breathing. No pushing, cutting the course, or blocking.

Vocabulary: pace, talk-test, lap, mile, recovery, finish time.

Official mile targets (min:sec, at most) for common 3-5 ages. Look up other ages in benchmarks.md. Age 5 has no official number.
Boys Presidential / National: age 8 = 8:48 / 11:05; 9 = 8:31 / 10:30; 10 = 7:57 / 9:48; 11 = 7:32 / 9:20.
Girls Presidential / National: age 8 = 10:02 / 12:30; 9 = 9:30 / 11:52; 10 = 9:19 / 11:22; 11 = 9:02 / 11:17.

Guided Practice (SW):
Whole class on the course. Coach times and watches safety.

Round 1 — Pace practice (6 min): 2-3 short intervals (about 1-2 minutes jog / 1 minute walk) on the course. Coach calls "talk-test" and "recovery walk."

Round 2 — Build (8-10 min): Continuous jog/walk toward a half mile or more, based on the group. Students may walk recoveries but must stay on the path. Faster runners pass on the outside with space.

Round 3 — Timed practice (as time and readiness allow): students ready for a full mile may complete it; others run a posted number of laps toward one mile. Coach calls elapsed time as finishers cross. This is not the official test.

Water mid-lesson if it is warm. No racing for a grade — effort and pacing count.

Cool-down:
Walk at least one easy lap. Calf, quad, hamstring, and shoulder stretches (3-4 min).

Kagan/SLAMified — Timed Pair Share:
After the walk, stand with a partner. Round 1 (30 sec): "One pacing tip I used today was ___." Round 2 (30 sec): "On test day I will try ___." Switch who talks first.

Exit Ticket:
Thumbs-up/sideways/down: "Did I start under control and finish without sprint-and-crash?" Coach notes students who cut the course or sprinted the first lap — 1:1 pacing reminder next class.

Reminders:
Check heat, hydration, and footwear. Have a shaded rest option. Students who feel dizzy, overheated, or cannot breathe well walk or stop and tell the coach.
Same direction for everyone. Listen for the freeze signal. Confirm lap count before official testing week.
